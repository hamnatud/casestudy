# First Viewpoint Analysis: The Societal Perspective
First Viewpoint Analysis: The Societal Perspective
A sociological approach to the shift from Nokia phones to the iPhone would require one to consider what people did with the phones, and how their everyday life changed as a result. It's not about the hardware, it's about the habits that are formed around it.
## From a phone to a social tool
Initially mobile phones were purchased for calling out from home or office. This quickly changed when technology was introduced to send texts. The first SMS was sent in December 1992 and in the year 2000, texting was already a normal part of life for many people, particularly teenagers, with phones such as the Nokia 3310 available. The use of mobiles by young people for what Ling (2004) calls micro-coordination, changing plans at the last minute, saying they were running late, and working out where friends were, was studied in Norway. There was an increase in social freedom. The fixed timing and location of meetings was less important because people were always available to contact on the move. The same study indicates a change in the pattern of family life. Now a parent can communicate directly with a teenager, bypassing the household phone system, thus giving teens privacy and parents a way to monitor. The same device was used to give each side something.
