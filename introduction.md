# Introduction
Mobile technology has undergone a massive evolution from the days of Motorola and Nokia to present day iPhones. The mobile phone market was initially dominated by Nokia in the 90s and early 2000s, with releases such as the Nokia 1110 and 3310 selling hundreds of millions of units across the globe, including in developing countries. These devices were praised for their low price, durability and long battery lives which at the time, was exactly what people wanted in their mobile phone.

However, this all changed when Apple released the original iPhone in 2007. It featured a revolutionary capacitive touchscreen, changing the way people interacted with their phones forever. It also included a multimedia player and a web browser, becoming an all-in-one device that functioned more like the computers and laptops of the time than a mobile phone. Mobile phones have evolved from devices only capable of voice communication and SMS texting to all-in-one computers that have revolutionized the internet and made it an essential part of our lives. Mobile technology is getting more advanced as time goes on, with current flagships being orders of magnitude more capable than the Nokias of the previous decade.

In this case study, we aim to track the history of mobile technology and analyse its development along two viewpoints, from the first mobile phone to present day smartphones. We will analyze the impact that this evolution has had on both general society, such as access to the internet, online shopping and social networking. We will also analyse the impact it has had in the business world, such as improvements to communication in and between companies, the rise of remote work and blurring of work-life boundaries. Finally, we will evaluate the ethical implications that the evolution of mobile technology has had so far and what it may lead to in the future.
