# Introduction
Mobile technology has undergone a massive evolution from the days of Motorola and Nokia to present day iPhones. The mobile phone market was initially dominated by Nokia in the 90s and early 2000s, with releases such as the Nokia 1110 and 3310 selling hundreds of millions of units across the globe, including in developing countries. These devices were praised for their low price, durability and long battery lives which at the time, was exactly what people wanted in their mobile phone.

However, this all changed when Apple released the original iPhone in 2007. It featured a revolutionary capacitive touchscreen, changing the way people interacted with their phones forever. It also included a multimedia player and a web browser, becoming an all-in-one device that functioned more like the computers and laptops of the time than a mobile phone. Mobile phones have evolved from devices only capable of voice communication and SMS texting to all-in-one computers that have revolutionized the internet and made it an essential part of our lives. Mobile technology is getting more advanced as time goes on, with current flagships being orders of magnitude more capable than the Nokias of the previous decade.

In this case study, we aim to track the history of mobile technology and analyse its development along two viewpoints, from the first mobile phone to present day smartphones. We will analyze the impact that this evolution has had on both general society, such as access to the internet, online shopping and social networking. We will also analyse the impact it has had in the business world, such as improvements to communication in and between companies, the rise of remote work and blurring of work-life boundaries. Finally, we will evaluate the ethical implications that the evolution of mobile technology has had so far and what it may lead to in the future.

# The Societal Perspective: First Viewpoint Analysis

From a social perspective, the transition from Nokia handsets to the iPhone involves not just the technology itself, but the implications for daily life, so thinking about the transition requires a reflection on what people did with these devices and how things changed for them. The hardware is not as important as the habits that developed on it.

## From a phone to a social tool

The primary reason for the purchase of the initial mobile phones was to call out from the home or office. This is all about to change when text messaging became popular. The first SMS was sent in December 1992 and by 2000, with the launch of the Nokia 3310, texting had become a normal part of life for many, particularly teenagers. They used it to alter plans at the last minute, find out where friends were etc. Meeting times and locations were less important because anyone on the way could be reached. It also influenced the lifestyle of the family. Additionally, a parent might now text their teen directly, rather than calling the family land line phone number, giving teens some privacy and parents some choice. ## Phones in the developing world

Things are different in rich countries. Landlines were uncommon in much of sub-Saharan Africa and a mobile phone was often the first telephone to be owned in a household or business. (Suri, Bharadwaj and Jack, 2021) conducted a study on M-Shwari, a digital loan service in Kenya which is accessed through mobile devices. They discovered that such loans enabled households to deal with unexpected circumstances, like a sudden drop in earnings, without reducing their expenditure by as much. Such services evolved from mobile money services like M-Pesa, and many of them operate on cheap, rugged, days-long holding basic phones. That sort of phone did more good to many users than an expensive cellphone.

## The smartphone shift

The iPhone was released in 2007 and the App Store was released in 2008. From then on, the phone was used primarily for calls. It is now a camera, a map, a music player, a bank, a news feed and a social network in one pocket. Since people were taking it with them all day, they were using it throughout the day rather than in brief periods at a desktop. Some studies estimate that this had no effect on people.Some studies suggest that this had no impact on people. (Allcott et al, 2020) conducted an experiment to make approximately 2,700 users in the United States of America delete their Facebook accounts for four weeks. Those who did not log on online experienced less online time, more time with friends and family, and experienced slightly more overall well-being. (Haidt, 2024) takes it one step further. He believes the transition to the "smartphone childhood" in the early 2010s has hurt the mental health of teens, and that parents and schools should restrict teens' phone usage. But other researchers question the strength of that evidence, and his assertion should be considered a part of a debate. There is little question that communication now became ubiquitous, which altered the way people want to be communicated with by their friends, family, and employers. # The Business Perspective: Second Viewpoint Analysis

## Nokia's Dominance in the Mobile Phone Industry
During the 1990s and early 2000s Nokia became one of the most successful companies . Nokia became very popular in the 1990s, specifically after launching the Nokia 2100 series in 1994, which featured the iconic ringtone and sold 20 million units. And then in the Year 1998 it became the world largest phone manufacturers, selling approximately 40.8 million phones that year (Nokia, 1998). Its success was driven by its reliable products, affordable prices and ability to reach customers in different countries. One of Nokia's most popular phones was the Nokia 3310, which became well know in the 2000s for its durability, long life and simple design and sold 126 million of this phone (www.mobilephonehistory.co.uk, n.d.).

Nokia's success demonstrated how a company could become a market leader through product quality, innovation and effective business strategies. However, as technology developed and customers began demanding more advanced mobile devices, Nokia faced increasing competition from companies introducing smartphones with touchscreen technology and improved software.*

## Apple's Entry into the Mobile Phone Market

In January 2007, Apple entered the mobile phone industry by introducing its first iPhone, officially announced by Steve Jobs on 9 January 2007. This was a major development because Apple introduced a new approach to mobile phone design and functionality.

Unlike many existing mobile phones, the iPhone combined three main features into one device: a mobile phone, a widescreen iPod and an internet communication device. It also introduced multi-touch touchscreen technology, allowing users to control the phone using their fingers instead of relying mainly on physical buttons and keypads (Apple, 2007).

From a business perspective, Apple's introduction of the iPhone created significant competition in the mobile phone industry. Apple focused on combining hardware, software and digital services to provide a better customer experience. This attracted customers who wanted more advanced technology and demonstrated the importance of innovation and understanding customer needs.

## Nokia vs Apple
Following the introduction of the iPhone in 2007, competition in the mobile phone industry increased significantly. Before Apple's arrival, Nokia was one of the leading mobile phone manufacturers worldwide. However, Apple introduced a different business strategy by focusing on touchscreen technology, software development and customer experience.

One major difference between the companies was their operating systems. Nokia mainly relied on Symbian, while Apple developed iOS for its iPhones. As customers increasingly demanded better applications, touchscreen interfaces and improved internet experiences, Nokia struggled to adapt to these changes. Apple gained an advantage by offering a more integrated software and application ecosystem (Doz, 2017).

Nokia's decline was not caused by Apple alone. Internal management problems, slow decision-making and difficulties developing its software also contributed to its challenges. In 2011, Nokia partnered with Microsoft and adopted Windows Phone as its main smartphone operating system. However, this strategy failed to restore Nokia's previous position in the smartphone market. In 2013, Microsoft announced its acquisition of Nokia's mobile phone business (Doz, 2017).

## Changes in  Business Models and revenue
The development of smartphone greatly changed the way technology companies generated revenue in the past. In the past, mobile phone manufacturers were mostly dependent on sell physical devices to their customers. But, when they smartphone were introduced, they created new business opportunities through application stores, digital subscriptions, advertising and online services.

One major development was the introduction of Apple's App store in 2008, which allowed developers to create and distribute apps to iPhone users. This created opportunities for businesses and developers to generate income through paid applications, in-app purchases and subscriptions. Apple also generates revenue through services such as Apple Music, iCloud and digital advertising (Sec.gov, 2025).

Smartphones also helped to create new opportunities for businesses to reach customers through mobile advertising, online shopping and digital services. Companies could generate additional income without depending entirely on physical product sales. Overall, smartphones transformed the mobile phone industry into a wider digital business market involving hardware, software, applications and online services.
 
## Impact on the Mobile Phone Industry

The evolution of mobile phones into smartphones has had a significant impact on businesses and the global economy. Smartphones have transformed from basic communication devices into important tools for business operations, communication and financial transactions. The industry has also become more competitive, with companies such as Apple, Samsung and Google constantly developing new technologies to attract customers.

One major impact has been the growth of online shopping and mobile banking. Customers can now purchase products, transfer money and make payments directly from their smartphones. This has allowed businesses to reach more customers and provide services more conveniently. According to Group (2025), mobile phones are increasingly being used for digital financial transactions and payments.

Smartphones have also changed how businesses communicate and advertise their products. Companies can use mobile applications, social media and online platforms to interact with customers and promote their services. The GSMA (2026) reports that mobile technologies and services contributed approximately $7.6 trillion to the global economy in 2025. This demonstrates the important role mobile technology now plays in modern business and economic development.

Overall, the transition from traditional mobile phones to smartphones has created new business opportunities, increased competition and changed how companies interact with customers.
